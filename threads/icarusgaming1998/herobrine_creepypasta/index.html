Before I start, let me make something extremely clear: I do not believe in the existence of Herobrine.


I’ve been a Minecraft fan since the game came out. Dug through hundreds of game files, ran worlds for hundreds of hours, tested mods linked to him and watched every alleged ‘sighting’ video with his name attached. In all that time, I have never seen a single piece of substantial evidence that points towards his existence. Everything is able to be explained away by mods, editing, bugs, misunderstandings or a person’s need for attention.


Though, that doesn’t mean Herobrine as a concept isn’t interesting. I actually do think his ‘story’ of sorts is highly misunderstood by the community, so I hope that my post helps iron out some misconceptions people have had regarding him in the past.


HIS STORY


In 2010, an anonymous user submitted a strange post on the /x/ board on 4chan, recounting an event that had happened within their Minecraft world.


According to their post, they were playing on a single-player world with their render-distance turned down due to having an older PC. All was seemingly normal for them until they went exploring, to which they discovered another ‘Steve’ player enveloped in the fog. As they approached, the figure disappeared.


So, they continued playing as if nothing had happened.


They then went on to claim that they discovered strange structures throughout their world after this encounter. Perfect tunnels, trimmed trees, unnatural generation and more. Each time they went on to speak about this online, their threads would ‘mysteriously vanish’. Odd, but probably just a coincidence. Right?


Wrong.


One day, the original poster received a private message from a user named ‘Herobrine’ with the simple word ‘stop’, hence the entity was finally given a name.


MY THOUGHTS


I personally believe that the reason why people thoroughly believe in the story and report sightings themselves is because Minecraft accidentally creates the perfect conditions for him to exist in our imaginations.


When we are alone, whether in real life or in a game, something happens; our brains are hard-wired to assume that the event(s) was caused by an agent. Agents in this context refers to people, animals, predators etc. It was these assumptions that kept us alive when we were susceptible to being hunted down


This phenomenon is called Hyperactive Agency Detection. So, whilst we are trekking through our worlds, our brain attempts to subconsciously look for other players so we do not fall short if a potential ‘threat’ appears. We have to remember that our bodies and brains do not understand the difference between real and fiction, so we will still regress into these primitive habits whether or not anything is at stake.


So, of course, once the story became widespread; people began to automatically assume that those unexplainable things in their world were something much more sinister than they actually were. This widespread panic was not helped by the fact that ‘Removed Herobrine’ began to appear in Minecraft’s changelogs in 1.6.6, which furthered theorists' belief in his existence.


Now, do I think the people who believe in Herobrine are stupid?
No.


Do I think they should’ve researched more before spreading these rumours?
Yes. It would’ve stopped a lot of widespread panic.
