Okay, my usual disclaimer before I start writing any of these posts, I yet again believe that Entity 303 does not exist. Do not come to me with blurry screenshots or statements from your younger cousin who apparently saw him because I will simply not respond.


He is not hiding in Minecraft’s code, he is not a former Mojang employee and I do not believe one in every forty Minecraft players is being secretly targeted by an international group of Minecraft hackers.


That being said, I have combed through a lot of the most commonly repeated stories, alleged sightings and warnings that all link back to Entity 303. Proves if that you actually do the research, the legend becomes less and less consistent.


Then again, that doesn’t mean that he isn’t worth looking into. Not because he might be real, but because he represents a very specific point in Minecraft’s history, i.e when the community attempted to manufacture a replacement for Herobrine.


HIS STORY


Entity 303, usually shortened to simply ‘303’, is described as a humanoid figure dressed in white. Most depictions gave him a white hooded robe or parka, a completely black face and red glowing eyes. This, of course, coined him the nickname of ‘the new Herobrine.’


Unlike Herobrine, who was originally presented as an unexplained figure appearing in a single-player world, Entity 303 was given a much more elaborate backstory. Most claim that he was once a Mojang employee, disgruntled by the fact that Notch himself had decided to fire him. So, of course, he decided to take revenge by hacking Minecraft itself for the sole purpose of attacking the playerbase.


Some versions have him as himself, others claim that it is a hacking group led by said employee.


That being said, here is the first contradiction. The story cannot decide whether its main villain is a single person or an entire organisation.


Nevertheless, the legend claims that Entity 303 and his hackers can enter private servers, corrupt worlds, alter game files, appear in crash reports and damage players’ computers. Some people even claim that one in forty players are affected by this ‘hack.’ Then again, there’s no source to prove this.


Anyhow, onto his alleged first sighting.


The earliest major story is traced back to an email apparently sent in December 2013. The email was supposedly written by a Minecraft player named Frankie and sent to a user known as TheSpeed179, who later began investigating Entity 303 publicly. According to Frankie, he regularly played Minecraft after school with three friends; John, Vincent and Brady.


The four of them would play on John’s private survival server and had spent around eight months building a desert city named Drybones. One day, whilst the group were gathering materials for a hotel in said city, John noticed something unusual in server chat. The server used both Essential and a plugin called SocialSpy. SocialSpy allegedly displayed an unknown user issuing the following command:


/stop


When investigated further, the username who ran this command read as ‘player.number

’. This was immediately treated as suspicious because the server was not only private, but it had a whitelist. Nobody but the four friends should have been able to join.


Naturally, Frankie assumed it was a bug. Though, twenty minutes later, that quickly changed. He and Vincent travelled towards a forest to collect wood, but as they crossed the desert, they reported that they saw a white figure standing in the distance. ‘Ghost-shaped’ with red eyes, though they couldn’t investigate as it disappeared before they could properly comprehend what was going on.


It continued to escalate. Strange activity was supposedly recorded by the server, players vanished and accounts were eventually compromised. Even Frankie claimed that he got a message saying the following:


‘I replaced him. He has died. He is no longer with us, nor anyone else.’


MY THOUGHTS


Okay, so I think I am going to slowly dissect this story point by point to explain exactly why this creepypasta is nothing but some faux fun.


THE FIRED MOJANG EMPLOYEE — The most famous explanation for the figure is that it’s an angry ex-programmer who decided to use his knowledge to destroy the experience of Minecraft for many. This is honestly the biggest reach, in my humble opinion. A former Mojang employee would not need to transform himself into a red-eyed figure and stand in the desert to scare some little kids. Instead, if he truly wanted to attack a Minecraft server, he would use ordinary vulnerabilities, stolen passwords, malicious plugins, compromised jousting accounts or simple social engineering (i.e faking joins etc).


Besides, there is no evidence that this employee existed at all. No name, job title, date of employment or reason of dismissal has been raised. That and if this was truly the case, Mojang would’ve made a statement as they are legally required to protect the integrity of their users.


THE INABILITY TO AGREE THAT ENTITY 303 IS ONE PERSON OR A GROUP — Some versions attempt to explain the scale of the alleged attacks by claiming that Entity 303 is a team of hackers with suspicious looking usernames that are all variants of the original name, hence why each story involving the entity seems to change. This, of course, causes debate within forums (much like this one) regarding whether these players are a group, imposters or one singularity. If no one can agree on one thing as simple as this, then what is the point of believing that each story is earnest?


THE SOCIALSPY PROBLEM — The initial story depends heavily on the SocialSpy plugin, supposedly detecting an unknown user attempting to execute /stop, finally announcing the start of the legend. Plugins have the capability to have their logs either altered or recreated by anyone with server panel access, so all it could’ve taken was a few things to be edited and boom. Scary man. SocialSpy is not reliable when referring to this story and attempting to prove Entity 303’s existence.


THE MISSING FRIEND — This is actually the part of the story that I have the most issue with. So, purportedly, Vincent disappears and everyone supposedly immediately assumes that he is dead. Not only this, but their fears are ‘confirmed’ when the hacker finally claims to have ‘replaced’ him. If this was truly the case, there would be a police report to refer to or at least a news article as child murder isn’t exactly something that is taken lightly in the eyes of journalism. Missing children get reported on continuously, so why would it be any different for him? Honestly, this story reads as someone who had not done any research regarding how these types of things go in the real world.


ONE IN FORTY PLAYERS CLAIM — First of all, where does this statistic come from? Through scouring the internet, I have not been able to find a single survey with reliable participants and a big enough sample group to say that this claim should be taken as truth. That and it never specifies the qualifications to fit into the ‘affected’ group, so why should we trust this?


Overall, my opinion is that the story was created to contend with the popular creepypasta, Herobrine. Someone wanted to see something new in the horror community and decided to craft a story that enamoured the masses, despite arguably being rather low in quality when it comes to making actual sense. However, it is still something that myself and many other people enjoy for the sake of story.
