What's at the center of your horror mythos?

2016/03/22 at 14:05:37

I realised that a lot of great horror writers tend to develop a core theme around their work. Even when the characters and the horrors themselves change, there's still something tying it all together.


For example, HP Lovecraft. His mythos is founded on the unknown, and more particularly indifference. His work reeks of a deep paranoia that the universe just....doesn't care. That we are nothing but molecules on a spec of dust in a universe bigger than we could hope to understand.


Clive Barker and his hellraisers are a mythos about pain. Pain is one of the few guaruntees in life. Physical pain, by triggering nerve endings, emotional pain, through grief, heartbreak, betrayal, rejection, mental pain, through abuse or boredom or what have you. But no matter you who are, you will feel pain at some point. Every decision you make is a fight against pain. Working a job? Money for shelter, food. Playing a video game? Avoiding boredom. Pain is a god in the eyes of Clive Barker, an eldritch horror that is the most motivating force in existence.

I think Ive finally figured out where my own mythos sits. Recursion. Ouroboros, reincarnation, fractals, the singularity, all the same concept that I cant stop fucking writing about lmao. Infinity, tucked within a finite package.
Importantly, its not eternity or infinity that Im talking about. I specifically mean infinity stuffed inside a finite thing.


Ouroboros is just a snake. A snake with a head and a tail. A line can be drawn. But the snake loops forever.


You are born, you live your life, and you die. A clear beginning and end point. But when you reincarnate, the cycle starts again. Being endless is a quality recursion often possesses, but its not the defining feature.


A fractal is a shape that has a finite area or volume, but infinite surface area. Theres an upper bound, the shape is only so large, but you can zoom in forever. An infinite amount of time will reveal an infinite amount of detail, despite being a finite object.

I cannot lie, I was stalking your profile a little after reading some of the works that you have posted and I couldn't help but respond to this.


I want to preface this with the fact that I thoroughly enjoy the distinction that you are making between infinity itself and infinity contained within something finite, as it feels a lot more specific than just being arrogant n flamboyant by just stating 'oh, my theme is eternity' like most writers like to do.


However, it is my duty to challenge you on a few things because apparently I am incapable of enjoying a bit of writing without tearing it to shreds.


In my humble opinion, I think you are slightly flattening Lovecraft to make them fit into singular concepts. An example is that Lovecraft definitely has some form of cosmic indifference that coats his writing, but I don't know if I would say the unknown by itself is frightening purely because the universe doesn't care about us and a lot of us have made peace with this. A lot of his horror, I would argue, actually comes from the fact that knowledge itself is corrosive. The majority of his characters in his tales don't just discover that humanity is insignificant, they also discover that their understanding of reality is fundamentally wrong.


This, like I stated, tends to be where the actual horror stems from rather than the vastness of the universe itself.


Anyway, that is all I wanted to comment. Sorry I decided to reply to this thread a few years later. : )
