What it means to be lonely.

2018/05/14 at 16:24:40

So, as I was drawing, I couldn’t help but notice how melancholic I had accidentally made the piece. It was initially meant to be a pose study featuring myself, but something about it had inspired me to do some writing alongside it. This is not Minecraft related, so feel free to scroll if you must. : )
—


What does it truly mean to be lonely?


Is it something as simple as absence; the empty space beside you, the voice that no longer answers when called. Or, is it merely another abstract terror hammered into our heads until we learn to perceive it as inherently bad? It has never sat right with me, this insistence that all loneliness must be a form of suffering.


You could wander the eternal halls of purgatory, passing door after door without ever finding another soul behind them, and still remain content within your own skin. Your footsteps might be the only sound left in that endless place, but at least they would belong to you. At the end of it all, what truly matters is autonomy. The quiet certainty that your thoughts are your own, that your hands move because you command them to, that no one else has the right to decide what shape your life must take.


This is why it vexes me when others claim we must belong to another when that is certainly not the case.


We may offer pieces of ourselves. Our time, our trust, the softer parts we usually keep hidden; but no person can ever be promised to another.


People leave.


Sometimes through choice, sometimes through cruelty, and sometimes with such terrible suddenness that the warmth has not yet faded from their chair before the world begins referring to them in the past tense. One moment, they are speaking. The next, their mouth is still, their eyes fixed upon something no one else can see and every word you thought you still had time to say becomes useless inside you.


That is the fragility of surrounding yourself with others. You mistake their presence as permanence.


A coat left hanging by the door does not mean its owner will return, you know? The dead leave ordinary things behind and perhaps that is what makes death so obscene. Nothing announces itself. Clocks continue and rooms remain, the only difference being that the person is missing, excised so cleanly from the world that their absence begins to feel
more tangible than they ever did.


Yet, here you are. Left behind.


You are the only person you are guaranteed to wake beside.


Perhaps loneliness is merely a lie bestowed upon those who have yet to discover that kingdom. A fear placed carefully inside them so they might be moulded by whatever environment surrounds them. After all, someone terrified of being abandoned can be made to endure almost anything. They will surrender their voice, their dignity, even the boundaries of their own body; provided someone promises not to leave.


We are social creatures by nature, of course. Some ancient part of us still understands isolation as a death sentence. If we were left to fend for ourselves by firelight, with no signs of those who hunt and gather or the mothers that would keep our bodies warm, we would surely succumb to the beasts of the night.


Funny how we now have to fear other beasts.


Man is corrupt. Man stigmatises what he cannot understand and shames whatever obstructs the finality of his blueprint. He calls solitude sickness because he cannot tolerate the possibility that another person might not need him. He teaches us to fear an empty room, when perhaps what truly frightens him is someone who can sit within it undisturbed.


I do not wish to be lonely.
I only think people should become more comfortable with absence. With empty chairs, cold bedsides and the possibility that those they love may be taken without warning.


There may come a day when every familiar voice has fallen quiet. When I walk through rooms once crowded with life and find that only my footsteps answer me. When there is no hand left to reach for and no one remaining who remembers the person I used to be.


But even then, I will have myself.


And surely, so long as I remain my own, I cannot truly be alone.
