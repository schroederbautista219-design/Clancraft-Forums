reunion

00/00/00 at 00:00:00

I thought I had lost her.


Well, perhaps I still have.


What remains of her truly holds no weight; only a stumbling congregation of shadow, gathered vaguely into the shape of what used to be her. At times, she turns towards me with something almost like recognition. Hollowed out eyes settled upon my face and for once merciful instant, I believe she is still there. That she knows me.


Then her jaw hangs loose and the illusion rots.


She is imprisoned within a carcass assembled all wrong. Every movement resembles a blasphemous imitation of life. Body twitching beneath ruined skin, each tremor a reminder that her body bestows a burial shroud filled with flies. Whatever found her must have been brutal. There is so little left that I recognise, save for the tufts of frizzy hair clinging stubbornly around her disfigured face.


A tarnished halo.


I once thought I was a religious man. Truly, I did. I believed that there was intention behind suffering, that every wound was another line of scripture. But there is no scripture for this.


What loving God would permit such a thing to kneel at the altar of His creation and feed? What divinity could watch something hollow her out, wear her likeness and drag her back to me like a cruel answer to a prayer.


Maybe this is resurrection and we had misunderstood the promise.
I think I am losing myself too.
My thoughts no longer arrive in my own voice, something speaks for them. Something ravenous, deranged. It roots itself between my ribs, turning over each organ as if choosing from a banquet.


My innards are it’s feast.
My blood is it’s wine.
My flesh is it’s bread.


Yet, no communion is being held. Nothing is shared. Nothing is sanctified. It takes and takes, swallowing every sacred part of me without gratitude or reverence. Only greed. Incessant, fucking greed.
I am not ready to forget her.


I am not ready

to
lose her
again.


So, I remain before her and she before me. Two abandoned offerings at the foot of an empty altar, waiting for
the other to remember what we once were.


A melancholic reunion, or something cruel enough to resemble one.
